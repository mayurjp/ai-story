Now you know the grown-up words

Tap one to see what it means.
Neuron +
Pixel, start to finish, in this exact story: a card comes in (), a few knobs act on it (), and one answer comes out the other side (). That whole loop — inputs in, weighing, one answer out — is what's called a neuron. Right now, Pixel is one.
Weight +
The knobs you saw turn in . Each knob controls how much one clue pushes Pixel's answer toward "C" or away from it — turn it up, that clue matters more; turn it down, Pixel starts ignoring it.
Threshold +
Pixel doesn't blurt out "C" the instant it sees the card — it adds up how much all its knobs are pushing it toward "C," and only answers once that total crosses a line. That line is the threshold. happens because the total didn't cross it yet; happens because, after the knobs got nudged, it finally did.
Perceptron +
The name for the entire card-in → knobs-adjust → answer-out machine you just watched. It's not just a teaching metaphor — this exact design was built for real in 1957, with physical, turnable knobs instead of the on-screen ones Pixel has.
Supervised learning +
What's actually happening in and : someone always tells Pixel the correct answer ("No — it's a C!"), and Pixel adjusts based on being told, rather than figuring it out alone. Training where you're always handed the right answer first is what gets called "supervised."
Classification +
What Pixel is doing this entire story: sorting the card into one of a small number of known categories — "C" or not — rather than inventing a brand-new kind of answer from scratch. Sorting something into one of a set of known categories like this is called classification.

Linear regression +
What Pixel is doing this whole time: instead of sorting something into a category like Chapter 1's "C" or not-C, it's predicting an actual number — how high the ball bounces — by fitting a straight line through examples, as it does by . Predicting a number this way, by fitting a line, is called linear regression.
Line of best fit +
The specific straight line Pixel ends up with in — the one sitting as close as possible to every dot at once, not just matching one or two perfectly. That closest-overall line is called the line of best fit.
Residual +
The gap between where a dot actually sits and where Pixel's line says it should sit — huge in , shrinking by . Each one of those individual gaps is called a residual.
Continuous prediction +
Chapter 1's Pixel could only answer "C" or "not C" — a small, fixed set of choices. This Pixel, by , answers with any number at all along a scale — 4 centimeters, 4.5, 12.7. Predicting a number like this, instead of picking from fixed categories, is called continuous prediction (regression, for short).

Step 1 of 5

Not Yes or No this time

Pixel learned to classify cookies in Chapter 1. But real life isn't just Yes/No! Today, Pixel is asked to predict a continuous number: "If we drop a ball from 8 feet high, exactly how high will it bounce?"

Now you know the grown-up words

Here is how these ideas power real-world AI:

📈 Linear Regression

Drawing a straight line through past examples to predict numbers (like house prices, temperature, or bounce heights).

📏 Line of Best Fit

The single best balanced line that passes as close as possible to all data dots at once.

↔️ Residual (Error Gap)

The gap between where a dot actually landed and what the line predicted. Training shrinks these gaps to zero!

🔢 Continuous Prediction

Predicting an exact measurement on a continuous scale (e.g. 4.2 feet) rather than picking a fixed label like Yes/No.

⚠️ What if you throw the ball into a basketball hoop?

When you shoot a basketball, it doesn't fly in a straight line—it arcs in a curve! A single straight line fails completely on curves. To handle curved real-world physics, Pixel needs layers of neurons that learn by working backward from mistakes. That is Backpropagation (Chapter 3)!

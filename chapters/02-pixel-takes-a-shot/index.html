Now you know the grown-up words

Tap one to see what it means.
Loss +
The "how far off" feeling from — the size of the miss, measured as a number. Pixel doesn't just know it missed; it knows exactly how wide the shot was. That number, how wrong a guess was, is called the loss.
Backpropagation +
What actually is: Pixel doesn't just re-aim randomly — it traces the miss backward through every part of the throw, from the hoop back to the shoulder, to work out which part caused how much of the error. Working backward from a mistake to fix the parts that caused it is called backpropagation.
Gradient descent +
The way each part gets nudged in , and again after : not one big random change, but a small step in whichever direction shrinks the miss a little more. Taking those small, error-shrinking steps over and over is called gradient descent.
Layers +
Pixel's throw in isn't one motion — it's a chain of stages, each feeding the next, each getting its own correction in . A network built from stages stacked like this, where each one adjusts based on what came before it, is what's called having layers.
Nonlinearity +
Notice in : a tiny wrist snap right before release changes the shot far more than turning the shoulder by that same tiny amount. The effect isn't a straight, even trade. That bent, uneven relationship between an adjustment and its effect is nonlinearity — without it, Pixel could only ever learn plain, straight-line corrections.
